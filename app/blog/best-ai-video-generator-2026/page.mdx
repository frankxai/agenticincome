## What actually separates the tools

Three things decide whether a video generator is worth building a business on: **quality**, **buyability**, and **support**.

**Quality** is what most reviews measure — motion coherence, artifact rate, how well a face holds up across frames. Sora, Veo, and Kling lead here more often than not. That's a real, measurable fact, not a hedge.

**Buyability** is whether a creator can actually get in. Higgsfield and Pika sell direct subscriptions with clear tiers. Sora and Veo are gated behind their parent platforms' access rules and pricing, which shift without much notice. Kling's access for Western creators runs through aggregators, not a direct consumer signup.

**Support** is what happens after you pay — a program built for repeat use (character consistency across a season of content, motion presets, an interface built around iterating fast) versus a one-off generation with no continuity between clips. This is where Higgsfield's actual product design shows: it isn't just a model, it's tooling built around a creator making video after video, not one impressive demo.

A tool can win on quality and still be the wrong buy for a business that needs all three.
