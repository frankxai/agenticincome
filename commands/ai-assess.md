# /ai-assess — Capability Audit

Run the **capability-audit** skill discipline. Input: `$ARGUMENTS` (may contain URLs to the user's existing surfaces).

## Process

1. **Surfaces.** If URLs given, fetch and analyze each: what it says they do, who it speaks to, evidence of traffic/engagement, what's monetizable about it today. If none given, ask for: website, X/LinkedIn/YouTube/Instagram, newsletter, GitHub, portfolio, anything with an audience or search presence.
2. **Skills.** Elicit concrete capabilities (what people have paid them for or thanked them for), tools they operate, domains they know deeply. Push past modesty — "what do people ask your help with?"
3. **Assets.** Existing content, code, templates, courses, lists, communities, past client results — anything reusable as raw material for offers or streams.
4. **Constraints.** Weekly hours, cash runway, risk tolerance, platform dependencies, jurisdiction quirks (only what matters practically), technical comfort with agent runtimes (knowledge/01 tiers).
5. **Leverage read.** Identify the 2–3 strongest capability×surface combinations — where existing distribution or proof already exists. These become the architecture candidates.

## Artifact

Write `income/00-capability-inventory.md`:

```markdown
# Capability Inventory — <name> — <date>
## Surfaces (with honest reach numbers)
## Skills (ranked by evidence people pay for them)
## Assets (reusable raw material)
## Constraints (hours/wk, runway, risk, tech comfort)
## Leverage candidates (2–3 combinations, one line each on why)
## Gaps (what's missing for each candidate — smallest bridge, not a curriculum)
```

End by summarizing the leverage candidates in 3 sentences and hand off: "Next: `/ai architect` to choose the model and build the plan."

Doctrine: honest numbers only (a 90-follower account is a seed, not a lie to round up); no flattery inflation; constraints are design inputs, not embarrassments.
