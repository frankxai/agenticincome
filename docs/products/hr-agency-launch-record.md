## Product contract

Buyer: experienced independent recruiter, boutique HR agency owner, or builder partnered with an HR practitioner.
Repeated job: turn fragmented recruiting tools into a scoped, costed and reviewable client service.
Present workaround hypothesis: manual coordination across ATS, email, documents, meetings and invoices.
Input: one niche, one role, permitted sources and a service budget.
Output: scoped service, evidence rubric, operating templates and scenario economics.
First acceptance: complete the fictional case with traceable evidence and a costed scope.
Product evidence: original artifacts and executable local calculator. Buyer acceptance, live integrations and willingness to pay remain unverified.

Based on the existing [IncomeSystem contract](https://github.com/frankxai/agentic-income-skills/blob/main/schemas/income-system.v1.schema.json), mapped in the kit. No new IncomeSystem schema or conflicting product identity is introduced.

## Price and unit economics hypothesis

Base kit: US$149 one-time before applicable tax; one agency and up to five internal users.
Future automation edition and Ana method module are separate proposals, not bundled promises.

Illustrative digital unit economics:
$149 gross − $10.43 assumed 7% refund/processing allowance − $30 acquisition − $37.50 support (45 minutes at $50/hour) − $10 update allocation − $3 delivery = $58.07 contribution before product development and unentered overhead.
At 90 minutes of support, contribution falls to $20.57. At $80 acquisition and 90 minutes of support, contribution is negative. These are synthetic assumptions, not observed costs, benchmarks or revenue.

Development budget is to be entered from actual time logs; it is not silently treated as free. Proposed recurring support cost ceiling is 45 minutes per activated buyer. A later update subscription requires a funded maintenance promise and evidence that buyers need it.

## Release criteria

1. Artifact integrity and calculator tests pass for the exact packaged source.
2. An HR practitioner accepts the case, interview boundaries and service scopes.
3. At least four of five proposed pilot buyers complete the exercise; record support time and corrections.
4. Approve final license, support destination, price/tax display and method attribution.
5. Upload the versioned archive to private merchant delivery, map the specific product, verify test purchase, receipt, file access and refund.
6. Build and verify the storefront at required desktop/mobile widths, then approve production promotion.

Checkout remains closed and the product is deliberately absent from the Polar environment-key map. The catalog delivery path currently opens the free preview; it must be changed to the verified paid-delivery destination before activation. Do not open checkout solely by editing the catalog status.

## Distribution experiment

Channel: one relevant practitioner community or existing professional audience, selected by the owner.
Action: use the free sample; then request the kit once genuine intake is configured.
Activation: completed practice case accepted by a domain reviewer.
Proposed population: 20 qualified practitioners over 30 days; target five paid pilots after delivery verification.
Stop/revise: activation below four of five, material candidate-evidence errors, or median support above 45 minutes.

No outreach, community posting, contact collection or payment was performed in this change.

## Implementation scope

The private kit, catalog entry, allowlisted MCP sample and deterministic packaging are implemented in this branch. The storefront document is page-ready copy, not a deployed visual page. Existing UI components remain unchanged.

The repository's web-release-gate requires mobile capture before UI changes. The supported cloud browser has no viewport-setting capability; visual implementation is deferred at that gate. Backend, product files and static sample delivery can be completed without misrepresenting visual validation.
