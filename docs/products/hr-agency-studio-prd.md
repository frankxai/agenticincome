# Agency Studio product and implementation record

ID: `hr-agency-launch-kit` · Release 0.2.0 · Owner surface: Agentic Income · 10 September 2026

## Problem and product outcome

The first kit covered one hiring-service ladder. It did not let an agency configure different services, distinguish permission from quality evidence, or connect the existing retreat work to a reusable commercial portfolio. This release turns that content into a local service configuration product with six complete service packs and reviewable co-selling terms.

Buyer: experienced recruiter or boutique HR agency owner; builders must partner with a competent HR practitioner. Job: turn current knowledge, coordination and human expertise into scoped services with accepted outcomes and sustainable delivery cost. Demand, price acceptance and actual savings are unmeasured.

## Shipped implementation

- Versioned service catalog: six services, thirteen workflow definitions, four architectures, five autonomy levels and three human service levels.
- Local CLI: catalog, configuration, offer generation and quality assessment. It reads explicit local JSON and emits text/JSON; no external action transport exists.
- Quality evaluator: exact scope/version/date, sample counts, independent/live evidence distinction, stale evidence exclusions, material-error override and historical critical-incident stop.
- Configuration: validated input, architecture/control/evidence caps, workflow-specific human authority and explicit disabled execution.
- Economics reuse: human delivery plus additional review, fees, refunds, tools, acquisition, setup, contingency reserve, capacity and doubled-human-time stress case.
- Six service packs with client brief, operating playbook, runnable example and generated example offer.
- Retreat and human-practice module, co-selling/ownership blueprint, pilot protocol, partner/connector/metrics templates and operating manual.
- Existing catalog identity retained; expanded Studio price hypothesis $499. Public sample updated through existing MCP allowlist. Checkout remains closed and paid files remain private.

## Acceptance

1. Every shipped service fixture generates a scoped usable offer and Q0/A1 plan when controls are unknown.
2. Increasing human service affects fee and delivery scope, not evidence rank or permission.
3. A4 proposals preserve human decisions and never enable transport.
4. Stale/wrong-scope/version evidence, small samples, failed controls and critical incidents cannot silently earn broader autonomy.
5. Invalid inputs fail with a nonzero CLI status; price arithmetic and stress outcomes are reproducible.
6. Clean archive extraction can run all tools with no network, credentials or installed package dependencies.
7. Public free delivery cannot expose paid source; checkout stays gated.

Local checks are recorded in the PR after execution. Model-produced documents and passing code tests do not establish practitioner acceptance or customer outcomes.

## Release limitations

No live customer account, hosted agent team, autonomous outreach, booking, payment or clinical service is implemented. Quality records are user-supplied and not authenticated. Connector and managed-network designs are specifications. The source product is locally usable but is not ready for paid launch until practitioner and commerce acceptance.

UI work is held by the repository `web-release-gate`: it requires mobile and desktop captures before visual editing, while the available supported browser API has no viewport control. The storefront copy is concrete; no new storefront UI, visual score or production promotion is claimed.

The 0.1.0 launch record remains historical. Current scope, price and support hypotheses are in the 0.2.0 product and license files. Existing IncomeSystem identity/mapping is retained; no new IncomeSystem schema or falsely validated instance is introduced.
