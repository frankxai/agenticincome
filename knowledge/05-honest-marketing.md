# Honest marketing: the trust-compounding playbook

The "make money with AI" category is a reputational superfund site. That is not a problem for us — it is the opening. When every competitor screams income screenshots, the operator who shows **machinery, provenance, and honest costs** is unmistakable. This file is both our compliance floor and our conversion strategy; they turn out to be the same thing.

## The legal floor (US/EU, re-checked September 2026)

1. **No earnings claims.** Not "make $10k/mo," not "results not typical" fine print under a Lambo. The FTC's **Operation AI Comply** (running since Sept 2024, active through 2025–26) has prosecuted exactly this — the 2025 [Click Profit action](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/ftc-acts-stop-click-profit-online-business-opportunity-has-cost-consumers-least-14-million) targeted baseless "AI-powered" passive-income claims, and AI-attached earnings claims are the agency's highest-risk category. Separately, the Consumer Reviews Rule bans fake or AI-generated reviews at up to **$53,088 per violation** ([FTC 2025 adjustment](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-publishes-inflation-adjusted-civil-penalty-amounts-2025)). Our standard is stricter than the law: **we do not market outcomes at all — we market machinery and education.**
2. **Affiliate disclosure, always, before the click.** Every surface carrying a compensated link labels it plainly ("we may earn a commission — here's our selection doctrine"), and our `go.` redirector sends disclosure headers on every hop. Disclosure lives next to the link, not in a footer.
3. **Material-connection honesty in content.** If we were paid, gifted, or partnered, the artifact says so at the top. If we haven't used a tool hands-on, the evidence label says "desk review" — the labels are part of the product.
4. **EU specifics:** consumer-facing digital sales need clear pre-contract information, withdrawal-right handling (your MoR does this — Slot 1 was load-bearing), and no dark patterns. **EU AI Act Article 50 has applied since Aug 2, 2026:** people must be told when they are interacting with an AI system unless that is obvious, generative outputs need machine-readable marking as AI-generated or manipulated, deepfakes must be disclosed, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be disclosed unless it has undergone human review or editorial control and a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for its publication. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since July 27, 2026) did not move that date; it only gives providers of generative systems already on the market before Aug 2, 2026 until **Dec 2, 2026** to meet the Art. 50(2) marking duty. Fines reach €15M or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (the lower of the two for SMEs), under Art. 99(4)(g). What the law itself asks of an operator like us is narrower than "label everything": the Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking duty sits with *providers* of generative systems; a deployer's own disclosure duty under Art. 50(4) covers deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest (exempt only when the text has had human review or editorial control and a named person or company holds editorial responsibility for publishing it); and if we offer an AI chat system ourselves, people must be told they are interacting with AI (Art. 50(1)). Our voluntary policy is stricter than that floor: because our content pipeline is agentic, we record AI-assistance provenance and label AI chat and AI media on every EU-facing surface. Sources: [AI Act Service Desk timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/timeline/timeline-implementation-eu-ai-act) · [Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) · [Article 99](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-99) · [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng) (all checked 2026-09-29).
5. **Testimonials/cases:** only attested ones (see [06](06-cases.md)) — dated, method-shown, consent-recorded, no compensation-for-praise. If a case shows revenue, it shows costs too.

## The doctrine above the floor

- **Recommendation survives the incentive.** Before any tool enters the registry with an affiliate link, it must already be recommended without one — and every recommendation names a credible non-affiliate alternative. If the alternative is better for a segment, we say so *first*.
- **Honest costs on everything.** Every stream, product, and pattern we publish carries its labor floor, latency to first result, and failure modes. This single habit converts better than any urgency timer ever built, because the reader can feel the absence of the con.
- **Show the machinery.** Our pipelines, registries, and workflows are built to be published as repos (as of 2026-09-28 they are still private pending launch). "Audit us" is the strongest CTA in this category and nobody else can afford to say it.

## What inspiring-and-honest sounds like (the voice)

Write toward capability, not toward dollars:

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Make $5k/mo on autopilot" | "Build a content engine that publishes verified work every week — here is every gear in it" |
| "Passive income secrets" | "Income structurally decoupled from hours — the mechanism, the costs, the kill criteria" |
| "AI prints money" | "An agent team compresses a three-week delivery into two days. Price the outcome." |
| "Join 10,000 winners" | "Install the pack. Run `/ai assess`. Ship something real this week." |

Kill list: *hustle, secret, unlock, effortless, 6-figure, autopilot, overnight, guru, ninja.* Power list: *machinery, architecture, attested, inspectable, compound, sovereign, ship.*

## The distribution playbook (earned, not bought)

1. **The knowledge base is the SEO.** Deep, dated, re-verified pages outrank thin listicles on the queries that matter — and get cited by LLMs, which is the new organic layer (structure headings as answers; keep facts dated and sourced).
2. **Open source is the funnel.** Packs and templates get installed, starred, forked — every repo README is a landing page (this one included).
3. **The Brief is the retention.** Weekly, factual, diff-driven ("what changed in the landscape + one teardown"). No launch-bro cadence.
4. **Attested cases are the proof.** One real, costed, consent-backed case beats a thousand testimonials.
5. **Syndication over posting.** Long-form lives here; agents cut it into platform-native formats (the `/api` pack's syndication loop) linking back. Own the canonical, rent the reach.
