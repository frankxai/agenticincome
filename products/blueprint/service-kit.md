## Who this kit is for

You have a skill a business already pays for — development, ops, content systems, data work, marketing plumbing — and you need revenue on a **days-to-weeks** clock, not months. Services are the only income model with day-scale latency to first cash. Agents can cut the hours a delivery takes; this kit makes sure that saving lands in *your* margin, not your client's.

**The one rule that decides everything:** price the outcome, not your hours. If you bill hourly, agent leverage cuts your invoice. If you price the outcome, agent leverage cuts your cost. Same work, opposite result.

## Part 1 — The business-plan template

```

Scope armor — explicitly excluded:   (the three things clients always ask for that you won't do)
Delivery window:                     (fixed days, not "2–6 weeks")

### 1.3 The delivery machine

Map your delivery to the five fulfillment patterns (`knowledge/03-agent-fulfillment.md` in the free knowledge base — [agenticincome.ai/pack](https://agenticincome.ai/pack)). Most first service offers need only Session + Team + Pipeline.

```

Build:            pattern = Team      artifact = deliverables in a private repo
Verification:     pattern = Pipeline  artifact = verification report

Aftercare:        pattern = Loop      artifact = 30-day check run log (optional, price it in)
```

### 1.4 The pipeline (how clients arrive)

```
Channel 1 (warm):     (your network — name 10 real people/orgs you can contact this week)
Channel 2 (proof):    (one public artifact per delivery — case note, teardown, template)
Channel 3 (earned):   (where your buyer reads; one contribution per week)
What you will NOT do: (paid ads before 3 delivered projects; cold spam ever)
```

### 1.5 Capacity and floor

```

## Part 2 — Worked example (fictional, complete)

> **This example is fictional.** "Mara" does not exist; the numbers are invented to be *realistic in shape*, not to predict anyone's results. It is here so you can see a completely filled template — the level of specificity that separates a plan from a wish.

**Mara — AI intake automation for regional insurance brokerages.**
Background: 6 years as an ops analyst at a mid-size insurer; can write Python; runs Claude Code on a Pro plan. No audience, no brand, needs revenue inside 45 days.

**1.1 Buyer:** Operations manager at independent insurance brokerages, 10–50 staff, US regional. Expensive problem: intake — new-client documents arrive as email attachments and get re-keyed by hand into the agency management system; ~15 staff-hours/week, error-prone, compliance-sensitive. They look for help in industry associations and peer referral, not on Upwork. Credibility bridge: Mara speaks the domain language and can name the exact systems (AMS360, Applied Epic). Not for: brokerages mid-migration between management systems (scope quicksand).

**1.2 Offer:** *"In five business days, your new-client intake runs through an agent pipeline that extracts, validates, and stages every submission for one-click approval — in your systems, with an audit log."* Included: one intake workflow, up to 3 document types, staging review UI, runbook, 2-week rework window. Excluded: AMS vendor API contracts, historical backlog processing, anything touching payment data. Window: 5 business days. Price: fixed — see scenario table. Acceptance: 20 real submissions processed with ≥95% field accuracy against manual re-key, checked by their staff.

**1.3 Delivery:** Day 0 discovery Session (60-min call → written brief, client signs). Days 1–3 Team build: researcher agent maps the document formats and AMS fields; builder agent writes the extraction pipeline; reviewer agent red-teams with malformed submissions. Day 4 Pipeline: end-to-end run on real (redacted) data, verification report generated. Day 5 handover Session: walkthrough, runbook, staff training call. Aftercare: 30-day weekly check Loop, priced into the fixed fee.

**1.4 Pipeline:** Warm: 14 ex-colleagues and 2 former vendors listed by name; 3 emails/week. Proof: one sanitized teardown per delivery published as a case note. Earned: answers intake/automation questions in two industry forums, one contribution a week.

**1.5 Capacity:** 2 concurrent max. ~14 focused Mara-hours per delivery. Agent spend per delivery: Claude Pro subscription share (~$20/mo covers roughly two deliveries at this intensity — verify your own usage against plan limits) plus ~$10–30 API/tooling. Floor: 1 delivery/month to continue.

### Mara's time ledger (the honest cost table)

| Phase | Mara-hours | Agent-hours (wall clock) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery + brief | 3 | 1 | The call is human work; the brief is drafted by agent, edited by Mara |
| Build direction + review | 6 | ~20 | Agents run long; Mara reviews checkpoints |
| Verification + fixes | 2 | 4 | Reviewer agent finds issues; Mara adjudicates |
| Handover + training | 3 | 1 | Trust is built here — do not delegate this |
| **Total** | **~14** | **~26** | vs. ~90–120 hours to build this solo in 2022 |

## Part 3 — Scenario economics

> **These are scenarios, not forecasts or promises.** Each column is an arithmetic worked example under the named assumptions. Change any assumption and the numbers change. No scenario is typical, guaranteed, or a claim about what you will earn.

**Named assumptions (all scenarios):** fixed-price delivery like the worked example; ~14 operator-hours per delivery; agent + tool spend $50/delivery-month averaged (subscription share + API — verify your own stack's pricing); no paid acquisition; taxes, insurance, and business overhead **excluded** (they are real — add yours); pipeline exists (warm network or equivalent). Price points are inputs you choose, not market data.

| | Scenario A — "First month" | Scenario B — "Finding rhythm" | Scenario C — "At capacity" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deliveries / month | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| Price per delivery (your input) | $2,500 | $3,500 | $4,500 |
| Gross revenue | $2,500 | $7,000 | $18,000 |
| Agent + tool spend | −$50 | −$100 | −$200 |
| Contribution before overhead/tax | $2,450 | $6,900 | $17,800 |
| Operator hours (delivery only) | ~14 | ~28 | ~56 |
| Hidden hours (sales, scoping, admin) | +10–20 | +15–30 | +25–40 |
| Failure modes to expect | No pipeline → 0 deliveries | Scope creep eats week 3 | Quality slips; referrals stop |

**Read the bottom row first.** Scenario A's most likely failure is not delivery — it's zero pipeline. Scenario C is not "more of B"; at 4 concurrent-ish deliveries the constraint becomes your review attention, and the fix is raising price, not adding volume.

## Part 4 — Fulfillment runbook (agent roles)

Run each delivery with this crew. Names match the `/ai` pack agents where they exist.

| Role | Agent or human | Responsibility | Quality gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Director | **You** | Scope, client contact, all approvals | Nothing ships without your review |
| Researcher | agent | Map client systems, formats, constraints → written findings | Findings cite sources; unknowns flagged, not guessed |
| Builder | agent (`delivery-engineer` prompt) | Implement to the brief in a private repo | Every commit references a brief line item |
| Reviewer | agent (adversarial) | Break the builder's work before the client can | Written defect list; zero-defect passes are re-run with a harsher prompt |
| Proof-writer | agent (`proof-writer` prompt) | Handover doc, runbook, sanitized case note | Passes both gates: engineer respect, FTC yawn |

**Non-negotiables (from the delivery quality floor):** brief before build · artifacts, not vibes · verification is a stage, not a hope · provenance (date + method + reviewer) on every artifact · cost telemetry per delivery in your ledger.

## Part 5 — Launch checklist

- [ ] Buyer section filled with a named, reachable segment (not a demographic)
- [ ] Offer written as a verifiable outcome with acceptance criteria
- [ ] Scope armor: exclusions list has at least 3 real items
- [ ] Price is fixed and outcome-anchored (no hourly anywhere)
- [ ] Delivery mapped to patterns; agent crew prompts saved in your repo
- [ ] One-page offer doc a client can read in 3 minutes (PDF or page)
- [ ] 10 warm contacts listed; first 3 messages drafted and sent
- [ ] Proposal → brief → acceptance templates ready (steal the worked example's shape)
- [ ] Invoice + simple contract ready (payment terms: 50% start / 50% acceptance, or 100% up front below ~$2k)
- [ ] Ledger open: revenue, hours, agent spend per delivery, from delivery #1
- [ ] Kill criterion written and dated (Part 6)
- [ ] First delivery scheduled with a 20%-lower "pilot" price and a testimonial/case-note agreement

## Part 6 — Kill-criterion worksheet

A plan without a stop condition is a zombie generator. Fill this in **before** launch, put the date in your calendar, and let the weekly `/ai operate` loop check it.

```
METRIC:        (e.g., signed deliveries)
THRESHOLD:     (e.g., 2 signed at full price)
BY DATE:       (e.g., launch + 60 days)
IF MISSED →    (choose one, in advance:)
   [ ] Kill: archive the offer, write the post-mortem, return to /ai assess
   [ ] Pivot: same buyer, different outcome (one pivot allowed, then re-date)
   [ ] Price test: 2 more weeks at −30% to test demand vs. price objection
REVIEWED BY:   (a person or the weekly loop — someone who will actually say "stop")
WHAT I WILL NOT DO: extend the date because "it feels close."
```

**Graduation criterion (the happy exit):** after 3+ deliveries with repeatable scope, take one week and package the delivery system — that's the [Product Kit](product-kit.md), and you'll enter it with the two things products need: proof and a pipeline.
