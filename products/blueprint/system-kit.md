## Who this kit is for

You already have income — from services, products, or a day job — and you are building the third thing: an asset whose yield is **structurally decoupled from your hours**. A publication, an evergreen product line with owned distribution, a disclosed affiliate property, a licensed catalog.

Two sentences of doctrine before anything else:

1. **"Passive" is a structure, not a nap.** Every real system has a maintenance floor — hours per month that keep it alive. This kit makes you compute and publish yours before you build.
2. **Systems are slowest to first dollar and highest ceiling.** If you need money soon, this is the wrong kit — go to [Services](service-kit.md).

The deep stream mechanics live with the siblings: [agenticpassiveincome.com](https://agenticpassiveincome.com) (`/api` pack — stream automation) and [disruptivepassiveincome.com](https://disruptivepassiveincome.com) (`/dpi` pack — portfolio governance). This kit is the *business plan* layer that decides whether a stream deserves to exist.

## Part 1 — The business-plan template

### 1.1 The asset thesis

```
The asset:                  (what exists after 12 months that didn't before — name the artifact)
The yield mechanism:        (exactly how it converts attention/usage into money — every gear)
Why it compounds:           (SEO + citations? list growth? catalog depth? What grows without your attention?)
Why YOU hold an edge:       (existing surface, domain knowledge, distribution seed — "I'll work hard" is not an edge)
The substrate you own:      (domain, repo, list — vs. what you rent: rankings, platform reach)
Platform-risk register:     (which single policy change would hurt most; your hedge)
```

### 1.2 The stream spec

```
Stream type:            (content+affiliate / newsletter / evergreen product line / licensed catalog / other)
The surface:            (site/publication/repo — the thing readers and models cite)
Revenue routes:         (rank them; a system should have ≥2 — e.g., affiliate + own product + list)
Disclosure posture:     (where the affiliate disclosure sits — next to links, before the click, always)
The non-affiliate rule: (every recommendation names a credible non-affiliate alternative; if the
                         alternative is better for a segment, say so first)
Maintenance floor:      (honest hours/month to keep facts fresh, links live, terms verified)
```

### 1.3 The loops (a system IS its loops)

```
Production loop:     (what gets made/refreshed weekly — by which agent, gated by whom)
Verification loop:   (fact/link/term re-verification cadence; terms drift while you sleep)
Distribution loop:   (syndication: canonical here, cut-downs there, links back)
Measurement loop:    (which numbers you read weekly; where the ledger lives)
Every loop:          idempotent · capped · observable · human-gated before publish
```

### 1.4 The registry (for affiliate/recommendation systems)

```
Programs tracked:      (tool, commission, recurring?, cookie window, network, verified-at date)
Source of truth:       (a versioned file — like data/programs.json in this repo — not a spreadsheet of vibes)
Drift policy:          (terms re-verified quarterly minimum; every change logged; stale = flagged)
Selection doctrine:    (recommendation survives the incentive, or the link doesn't ship)
```

### 1.5 Capital and floor

```
Cash cost to seed:        (domain, tools, MoR — usually small; the real cost is below)
Hours to seed:            (honest estimate to first complete surface — the worked example took ~60)
Monthly maintenance:      (the floor from 1.2 — publish it on the property itself)
Funding source:           (which Model 1/2 income pays for this until it yields — systems are bought
                           with earlier-stage revenue, not hope)
```

## Part 2 — Worked example (fictional, complete)

> **This example is fictional.** "Ana" does not exist. The numbers are invented to show realistic shape and honest mechanics — they are not projections, and affiliate income in particular is volatile and slow to start. Nothing here is an earnings claim.

**Ana — "The Voice Stack Ledger": a comparison property for AI voice tooling.**
Background: Ana ships a $39 podcast-production template pack (Model 2, ~15 sales/mo scenario-class volume) and did podcast-edit services before that (Model 1). Her buyers constantly ask which voice tools to use. She owns the question already — now she builds the asset that answers it.

**1.1 Thesis:** A deep, dated, re-verified comparison surface for AI voice tools (TTS, cloning, dubbing, editing), plus a monthly "what changed" email. Yield: disclosed affiliate routes + her own product + list growth feeding both. Compounds because voice-tool facts decay fast — a property whose facts are *provably fresh* wins the queries and the LLM citations that thin listicles lose. Edge: two years of real production use; an existing list of 900 template buyers/leads (fictional seed). Owns: domain, repo, list. Rents: rankings. Platform risk: a search-layout change halves organic traffic → hedge is the email list and the pack's install base.

**1.2 Spec:** Content + affiliate + own-product stream. Surface: 12 cornerstone comparisons + a public, versioned program registry. Revenue routes ranked: (1) her own $39 pack, (2) disclosed affiliate links, (3) list-driven launches. Disclosure sits beside every link with a one-line doctrine statement. Non-affiliate rule enforced in the template: every pick names a non-affiliate alternative first-class. **Maintenance floor, published on the site: 6 hours/month** (fact re-verification, link checks, registry diffs, one refreshed page).

**1.3 Loops:** Production: one page written or refreshed per week — researcher agent drafts the diff, Ana gates. Verification: monthly agent Loop re-checks prices/terms and files a PR ("terms drift is a bug"). Distribution: canonical article → agent cuts newsletter section + two platform posts linking back. Measurement: weekly 20-minute review — traffic, list growth, clicks by program, product sales.

**1.4 Registry:** Tracked in a versioned JSON with `verifiedAt` per program. Example rows from the July 2026 field dossier (re-verify at signup — programs change without notice): ElevenLabs 22% for first 12 months (11% on Business plan), 90-day cookie, via PartnerStack; Descript $25 flat per new subscription (cut from 15% recurring in early 2024 — the canonical drift lesson). Selection doctrine: she recommended both tools for two years before joining either program.

**1.5 Capital:** ~$150 cash to seed (domain, a year of small tools). ~60 hours to first complete surface (12 pages + registry + capture). Floor: 6 h/month. Funded by: pack revenue — the system is bought with Model 2 income while it spins up.

### Ana's seed ledger (honest cost table)

| Phase | Ana-hours | Agent spend | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registry build + verification | 8 | ~$10 | Every term traced to a primary source |
| 12 cornerstone pages | 36 | ~$40 | Agent drafts from her real usage notes; she rewrites the judgment |
| Capture + email wiring | 6 | ~$5 | The list is the hedge — wired before launch |
| Loops + measurement | 10 | ~$10 | The loops ARE the system |
| **Seed total** | **~60** | **~$65** | Then the floor: 6 h/month, forever or until killed |

## Part 3 — Scenario economics

> **These are scenarios, not forecasts or promises.** Affiliate and audience systems have the widest outcome variance of the three models — including the most common outcome, which is approximately zero for months. Each column is arithmetic under the named assumptions; none is typical or guaranteed.

**Named assumptions (all scenarios):** the worked example's shape (12 deep pages, monthly email, one $39 own-product route where product fees follow the Product Kit's assumptions); affiliate terms as in the July 2026 field dossier (e.g., ElevenLabs 22% first-12-months via PartnerStack) — **all program terms re-verified at signup**; conversion, traffic, and payout timing are the wild variables and are stated per column; maintenance floor 6 h/month held; taxes/overhead excluded; months 1–3 typically produce near-zero regardless of quality (indexing and trust lag).

| | Scenario A — "Months 1–4" | Scenario B — "Traction" | Scenario C — "Established surface" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly qualified visits (assumed) | 500 | 5,000 | 20,000 |
| Affiliate clicks (2% of visits) | 10 | 100 | 400 |
| Converting referrals (5% of clicks) | ~0–1 | ~5 | ~20 |
| Affiliate revenue (avg $8/referral/mo, 12-mo windows) | ~$0–10 | ~$40–120 | ~$300–600 |
| Own-product sales via surface (0.1% of visits) | ~0–1 | ~5 | ~20 |
| Product net (per Product Kit fee math) | ~$0–35 | ~$175 | ~$700 |
| Total scenario range / month | **~$0–45** | **~$215–295** | **~$1,000–1,300** |
| Hours / month | 6 (floor) | 6–8 | 8–10 |
| What must be true | Nothing yet — this is the latency | Pages rank/get cited; list ~1.5k | Surface is a reference; list ~6k; terms stayed favorable |
| Failure modes to expect | Quitting during the silence | A program cuts terms (Descript pattern) | Search-layout shifts; complacent freshness |

**The two honest readings:** (1) Scenario A lasting forever is the modal outcome for properties built without an edge or an existing seed — that is why section 1.1 demands one. (2) The decoupling is real: Scenario C's hours are barely above Scenario A's. That flatness — not the dollar column — is what "systems" buys you.

## Part 4 — Fulfillment runbook (agent roles)

| Role | Agent or human | Responsibility | Quality gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Director | **You** | Thesis, doctrine, every publish approval | Human gate before anything public |
| Researcher | agent | Draft pages/diffs from primary sources | Every fact dated + sourced; unknowns marked |
| Verifier | agent (Loop, monthly) | Re-check prices, terms, links; file PR | Idempotent, capped, observable; never edits live |
| Registry keeper | agent (Loop, quarterly) | Diff program terms vs. registry; log drift | Every change is a commit with a source |
| Syndicator | agent | Cut canonical → platform formats, links back | Disclosure intact in every cut |
| Ledger clerk | agent | Weekly numbers into the measurement doc | Numbers only from source APIs/exports, never estimated |

**Governance note:** once this stream is live, register it in your `/dpi` ledger and gate-check it quarterly like any other asset. A stream that escapes governance becomes a zombie with a maintenance bill.

## Part 5 — Launch checklist

- [ ] Non-affiliate alternative named for every planned recommendation

- [ ] Maintenance floor computed and published on the property
- [ ] All four loops specified: production, verification, distribution, measurement

- [ ] Ledger open; weekly 20-minute measurement slot in the calendar
- [ ] Stream registered in `/dpi` portfolio ledger with a quarterly gate date
- [ ] Kill criterion written and dated (Part 6)

## Part 6 — Kill-criterion worksheet

Systems need *two* stop conditions — one for the seed phase, one recurring — because their failure mode is slow rot, not loud collapse.

```
SEED GATE
METRIC:        (e.g., qualified organic visits/month, or list subscribers)
THRESHOLD:     (e.g., 1,000 visits/mo or 300 subs)
BY DATE:       (launch + 6 months — systems get more runway than products, not infinite runway)
IF MISSED →    [ ] Kill and post-mortem   [ ] Narrow the niche once, re-date   [ ] Demote to hobby (honest label, no maintenance guilt)

RECURRING GATE (quarterly, forever)
QUESTION 1: Did revenue per maintenance-hour rise, hold, or fall this quarter?
QUESTION 2: Are the facts on the surface verifiably fresh? (Spot-check 3 pages)
QUESTION 3: Would I build this stream today, knowing what I know?
TWO "no"/"fall" ANSWERS → schedule the kill/merge decision within 30 days.
REVIEWED BY:   (the /dpi quarterly gate — this is exactly what it exists for)
WHAT I WILL NOT DO: keep a stream alive because killing it feels like admitting the hours were wasted. The hours are spent either way; the floor is not.
```
