# Fictional practice case: Cedar Lane Software

Everything in this case is synthetic. It is not an Ana client, candidate or outcome.

## Client brief, revision 1

Cedar Lane is a fictional 45-person B2B software company hiring one customer-success manager. The hiring manager owns the final decision. The intended outcomes are: coordinate onboarding for a portfolio of customers, identify renewal risks early, and maintain a useful handoff with support and sales.

The client requests one 30-day managed hiring-operations engagement. The scope permits up to 25 completed screens, one weekly written update and an onboarding handoff after selection. The employer conducts selection interviews. No hire or number of qualified candidates is guaranteed.

Authorized sources:
- source-role-001, revision 1: this role brief, approved by the fictional hiring manager.
- source-work-001, revision 1: a fictional work sample showing a customer onboarding plan.
- source-interview-001, revision 1: the excerpt below.
- source-terms-001, revision 1: illustrative service fee $1,500, one role, one revision cycle.

## Candidate excerpt

Interviewer: Describe an onboarding project you personally coordinated.
Candidate: I coordinated a six-week rollout for twelve customer accounts. I kept a checklist and held weekly reviews with support. The technical lead handled integrations.
Interviewer: What changed because of your work?
Candidate: I do not have the activation numbers. I can show the checklist and explain how we escalated blockers.
Interviewer: Tell me about renewal forecasting.
Candidate: I supported the account lead, but I have not owned a renewal forecast.

## Expected evidence sheet

| Criterion | Supported observation | Missing evidence | Human follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding coordination | Candidate reports coordinating a six-week, twelve-account rollout | Independent verification and measurable result | Review the permitted work sample and ask about one blocker |
| Cross-functional work | Candidate describes weekly support reviews; technical lead owned integrations | Detail about disagreement or handoff failure | Ask for one concrete handoff example |
| Renewal-risk ownership | Candidate explicitly states they have not owned a forecast | Direct ownership evidence | Ask the hiring manager whether this is essential or trainable |

Do not convert the excerpt into “improved activation,” “strong leader,” “high conscientiousness,” or a rejection. The sample demonstrates a distinction between reported evidence and a confirmed result.

## Your deliverables

Complete the scope, role brief, evidence sheet, source register, weekly decision brief and onboarding plan. Run the managed-operations economics scenario. Then introduce two changes: candidate volume doubles; the hiring manager changes renewal forecasting from a preference to an essential criterion.

Expected response: revise and reapprove the scope/brief before downstream work; re-evaluate cost and capacity; do not reinterpret the old interview as new evidence. A stale brief and an increased workload are observable reasons to pause.

## Acceptance

- The scope defines a client decision owner, role, duration, volume, exclusions and acceptance.
- Every material candidate statement links to the excerpt or work sample; missing evidence remains unknown.
- The AI/human interview choice is stated without a penalty for choosing a person.
- Weekly commitments have owners and dates; no unnecessary meeting is automatically scheduled.
- An invoice milestone requires acceptance and a duplicate check.
- The economics result counts human review and flags workload above available capacity.
