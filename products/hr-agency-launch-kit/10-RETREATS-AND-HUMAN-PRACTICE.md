# Retreats, team practice and Ana's contribution

The retreat opportunity belongs in the same client journey: hire people, help them join, improve how they work together, and use a gathering when it creates decisions or artifacts that asynchronous work cannot easily produce.

## Reuse the retreat work already built

The existing [Retreat OS](https://github.com/frankxai/retreat-os) was reviewed at `fbfe4ee88263adae51c4084d29cb813092eeaef4`. Its README describes a fictional demo, event/application/booking state logic, a per-organization reference deployment, signed payment webhook routes that require configuration, and nine inactive workflow templates. We reviewed source documentation; we did not rerun that repository or establish live deployment readiness in this release.

The current retreat skills also provide planning, readiness, participant preparation, live support, closure and learning integration. Route work to those existing capabilities when installed and authorized. Their local schemas and evaluators are not a hosted booking service. Keep their original open-source rights and verify actual installed version before use.

## One complete team engagement

Use this fictional rehearsal: Cedar Lane Software has an eight-person customer-success team. Handoffs are unclear and weekly status meetings consume time. The sponsor wants a shared escalation agreement, one simplified customer-handoff workflow and an artifact each role can use within seven days.

Before: measure the existing meeting participant-minutes and coordination effort for two weeks; collect role-owned friction examples and functional participation needs separately. Compare asynchronous preparation and a local working day before a residential format. The commercial retreat service supports 8–20 people, but this rehearsal needs no travel.

During a local day: 30 minutes orientation and decision rights; 60 minutes mapping the current handoff; a break; 75 minutes redesign with structured dissent; lunch and quiet time; 90 minutes building the artifacts; a break; 45 minutes review and owner assignment. Add preparation and recovery time; do not fill every minute with facilitation.

Day 7: reviewers accept or return each artifact with specific corrections. Day 30: compare use of the agreements, accepted actions due, meeting time and active coordination effort against baseline. Satisfaction and attendance are separate observations. No causal productivity or wellbeing claim follows from a small pilot.

## Proposed Ana practitioner module

Ana chooses the parts she wants to author, review and deliver. Her neuroscience, psychology, psychotherapy and spiritual interests can inform the craft, but clinical treatment and optional personal practice remain separate services with their own appropriate records and consent. Do not describe her credentials or proven outcomes without source verification and her approval.

The distinctive human value can be careful listening, psychologically informed facilitation, attention to how people learn, room for meaning, and the skill to resolve a difficult conversation. These are concrete design choices to develop with her; they are not claims that a template treats illness or reads someone's inner state.

| Session card | Useful purpose | Original exercise draft | Accepted work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attention and arrival | Help people participate with focus | Offer two minutes of quiet reflection or silent reading, then let each person name one work intention | Participant-owned intention; no wellbeing score |
| Listening before advice | Reduce premature solutions | Pairs describe a handoff difficulty; listener reflects facts and asks what support is wanted | A mutually checked problem statement |
| Decisions with dissent | Make disagreement usable | Write proposals silently; name the decision owner; hear objections before choosing | Decision, rationale, owner and review date |
| Team working agreements | Turn preferences into explicit practice | Compare examples of helpful/unhelpful coordination without labeling personalities | Three observable agreements and a test period |
| Manager and new-hire partnership | Clarify support and expectations | Each drafts what good first-month work looks like and what remains unclear | Shared role plan and support commitments |
| Meaning and contribution | Connect work to voluntarily stated values | Invite private reflection on meaningful contribution; sharing is optional | Only the work commitment a person chooses to share |
| Integration after the event | Make insight become used work | Choose one small practice, trigger, artifact and review date | Day-7 artifact and day-30 use review |
| Closing and repair | Surface unfinished operational concerns | Offer a private support route and a separate public action review | Owned follow-up without exposing personal disclosures |

Every optional reflective or spiritual practice has an equal-status alternative. Neither attendance nor refusal is a hiring or performance signal. Employers receive accepted work and agreed operating outcomes, not private psychological or spiritual profiles. A clinical need goes to an appropriate qualified human; the agency tool does not diagnose or conduct psychotherapy.

## How human depth earns a premium

For the retreat service, H1 supplies reviewed preparation and client-owned facilitation. H2 adds defined practitioner delivery. H3 adds deeper preparation, leadership access, individual work where appropriate and follow-through. Price the actual hours, expert cost, support and liability exposure. If Ana prefers authoring and training other practitioners, the product can support that model without promising her recurring live availability.
