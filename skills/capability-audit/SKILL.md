# Capability Audit

The audit's product is not a list — it is the **leverage read**: the 2–3 capability×surface combinations where distribution or proof already exists.

## Method

1. **Surfaces first, with real numbers.** Fetch every URL they give. For each: audience size (honest), engagement evidence, search presence, what it could carry commercially *today*. A 400-subscriber newsletter in a tight niche outranks 20k drive-by followers — weight by trust density, not raw count.
2. **Skills by payment evidence.** Rank by what people have actually paid (or seriously thanked) them for — not by what they enjoy or credentials claim. Ask: "What do people ask your help with repeatedly?"
3. **Assets as raw material.** Old content, code, templates, client artifacts, datasets. Anything that shortens the path to an offer. Most creators are sitting on a product's worth of unshipped material.
4. **Constraints as design inputs.** Hours/week (real), runway, risk tolerance, tech comfort mapped to runtime tiers (knowledge/01). Never treat constraints as embarrassments — the architecture must fit them or it fails.
5. **The leverage read.** Cross skills×surfaces×assets: where do proof AND distribution already overlap? Score candidates by: existing-audience fit, payment evidence, agent-compressibility of delivery, time-to-first-artifact.

## Anti-patterns

- Inflating numbers to be kind (poisons every downstream decision)
- Inventorying aspirations as capabilities
- More than 3 leverage candidates (analysis theater)
- Skipping the "what have you already tried" question — prior failures are the cheapest data available
