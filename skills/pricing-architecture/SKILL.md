# Pricing Architecture

## The three regimes

1. **Services: price the outcome, never the hours.** Agents compressed a 3-week delivery into 2 days; hourly pricing donates that surplus to the client. Anchor to the value of the delivered workflow (what it saves/earns them per quarter) and the credible alternative (agency quote, hiring). State price as fixed-scope: one number, one scope, change orders beyond it.
2. **Products: the self-serve band is $19–$149 (2026).** Below $19 signals junk; above $149 buyers expect human contact. Within the band, price by depth-of-work-replaced: template/checklist $19–$47 · complete system/pack $47–$97 · multi-system kit with updates $97–$149. Updates-included beats subscription for trust at this scale — subscriptions only for genuinely recurring value (intel, refreshed data).
3. **Recurring: price the refresh, not the access.** $9–$29/mo self-serve ceiling. The subscriber must be able to point at what changed last month. Archive-access subscriptions churn to zero.

## The rules

- **One price, one optional premium tier.** Three-column pricing tables are conversion theater at solo scale.
- **No fake anchors, no countdowns, no "normally $499."** The honest version: launch price with a stated reason and a stated end.
- **Refund policy: generous and stated** (MoR handles mechanics). A 30-day no-questions policy costs less than the trust it buys.
- **EU/VAT: solved by MoR** — never price-discriminate by hiding tax; display MoR-inclusive behavior honestly.
- **Raise prices on evidence,** not vibes: >70% conversion on qualified traffic or delivery at capacity = underpriced.

## Sanity check

Before finalizing: find 2–3 live comparables (search), place the offer honestly among them, and write one sentence: "A skeptical buyer comparing us to X and Y would conclude ___." If the sentence needs spin, reprice or re-scope.
