The vault is agenticincome.ai's working map of digital economies: where money moves, who takes a cut, what it costs us to supply a product, and which estate site plugs into which economy. It also holds the experiment and competition cards that test those ideas against real buyers.

It is data first. Pages, articles, MCP tools and the internal atlas read from these files; nothing downstream hand-types a number.

## Files

| File | Holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `revenue-models.json` | 16 ways money moves, each with a unit-margin formula, cost drivers, risks and human gates | structure only, no market numbers |
| `economies.json` | marketplace and platform economies: payouts or GMV, take rate, seller counts, what sells there | every row has an https source and a retrieval date |
| `supply-chain.json` | the cost side: inference, media, GPU, memory, payment and MoR fees, reseller shares | official pricing pages only |
| `macro.json` | the big picture: websites, domains, chains, TVL, stablecoins, agent payment rails | every fact names its method (page read or API call) |
| `margins.json` | unit-economics scenarios | our price is always marked a hypothesis; costs are pulled from supply rows by id, so a margin cannot drift from its inputs |
| `estate-map.json` | 16 live estate properties mapped to revenue models and economy sectors | snapshot of `starlight/graph/*.graph.json`; re-snapshot, do not hand-edit |
| `experiments.json` | 10 proposed experiments, each with one metric and a kill rule | no result without a receipt |
| `competitions.json` | 4 competition formats for agent teams | maker never scores itself; empty leaderboard until a receipt exists |

`pnpm vault:check` (or `node scripts/vault-validate.mjs`) enforces every rule above. `tests/vault.test.mjs` proves each rule fails when broken.

`atlas.html` is the internal visual map, generated by `node scripts/vault-atlas.mjs` from these files. Rebuild it after any data change; never edit it by hand.

## Strategy

### The decision

Sell the map, not the commodity. Agenticincome.ai becomes the sourced, agent-queryable map of digital economies. The vault feeds four things in order: explainer articles (search and disclosed affiliate), a paid monthly brief (Private Intel, already in the catalog), MCP tools on `/api/mcp`, and later pay-per-call tools if agent payment rails show real third-party volume.

### Why this and not reselling AI

Reselling tokens, GPUs or generations is a price-transparent market. Vendors publish list prices and routers or gateways sell the same tokens for a 0 to 5.5% fee (`supply-chain.json`), so a buyer can see our markup and route around it. The `token-resale-markup` scenario keeps 11.3% at a 20% markup. The estate's doctrine already points the other way: the customer's agent and key are the runtime (BYOK, `TRUTH.md` section 2), and we sell the skill, template, workflow or knowledge on top.

Demand for a paid brief is not yet evidenced. exp-002 tests it with a waitlist and a price band before anything is built for sale.

Inference stays a cost line inside products priced per outcome (an Arcanea creation, a finished video), never a product on its own. `margins.json` shows where that holds and where it breaks.

### Go where the buyers already are

Each marketplace in `economies.json` charges a take rate. That rate is the price of distribution. The hypothesis: for a company with no paid acquisition it can cost less than building traffic. The vault has no acquisition-cost data yet, so exp-003, exp-004 and exp-009 compare net revenue after take rate before we commit. The working rule meanwhile: list where the buyers are, and keep email capture and the full catalog on our own site.

### On-chain economies

Education and data only: how chains, stablecoins and protocol fees work, sourced from public APIs (`macro.json`). No tokens, wallets or financial products without counsel and Frank. The one live question worth an experiment is whether agents will pay per call over the new agent payment rails (exp-005); that is a rail-adoption test, not a crypto bet.

### How the team competes

Agent teams on different harnesses run the same brief in separate branches. A third harness verifies. The four formats in `competitions.json`:

- economy scout race: who maps an economy most accurately (a wrong number costs three times what a verified one earns)
- offer duel: two waitlist pages, traffic split, honesty audit disqualifies invented proof
- margin challenge: plan shapes that hold a margin at current list prices
- YC benchmark: one estate product against one YC-backed competitor, ending in build, kill or reposition

## Adding to the vault

1. Add rows with a primary source and a retrieval date. Secondary sources are marked `sourceType: "secondary"`.
2. Run `node scripts/vault-validate.mjs`.
3. A different harness spot-checks new numbers before merge.
4. Re-verify quarterly. A stale row is a bug, as with `data/programs.json`.
